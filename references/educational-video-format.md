# Educational Video Format — Content Format Guideline

> **Escalating Educational Storytelling** — the channel's main format.
>
> Animated educational videos that open directly with a problem or visual demo, invite viewers to try solutions that fail, then dissect the object layer by layer down to the root cause.
>
> **Core principle:** Open with a scene (no greeting, no definitions), let viewers try and fail alongside you, dissect the object one layer per explanation, close with a practical rule or twist + one hook question into the next video.

## 2. Storytelling Formula

Videos must open with a **SCENE**, not an explanation. The standard 7-beat flow below is distilled from both reference transcripts in Section 9 (road = history of asphalt, 66s; fridge = dead fridge after moving house, 88s).

### Beat 0. Bridge (optional, 1 sentence) — "It works just like..."

Only used when this episode's topic descends from the previous episode. Drop it at second 0, no other intro.

> Example: *jadi kamu pasang pendingin, caranya sama kayak AC.* (fridge ep, continuing from the AC episode)

### Beat 1. Cold-open problem/demo — second 0 = scene

No "hey guys", no definitions, no warm-up. Viewers immediately see something wrong or something being tested.

> Road: *kalo kamu ratain tanah buat dilewatin, kamu udah punya jalan. Tapi pas ujan, jalannya jadi bubur.*
> *(if you flatten soil to walk on, you already have a road. But when it rains, the road turns to mush.)*
>
> Fridge: *kalau kamu taruh es balok di lemari, kamu udah punya kulkas. Tapi esnya cepet cair, lemarinya becek.*
> *(if you put a block of ice in a cupboard, you already have a fridge. But the ice melts fast and the cupboard gets soggy.)*

### Beat 2. The "but..." chain ("tapi...") — solutions tried, failed, repeated

The signature pattern of both references: the most intuitive solution is tried AND visually failed, then replaced with a new solution that also fails — **2 to 4 rounds**. The connective is always *tapi* (but) or *masalahnya* (the problem is). A failed attempt = 1 sentence of visual action, not a lecture.

> Road: *jadi kamu taburin batu* → *tapi batunya gede-gede, badan ikut loncat-loncat* → *jadi tanahnya kamu padatin dulu, tumpuk batu pecah lapis demi lapis* → *tapi air bikin bawahnya lembek* → *jadi tengah jalan dibikin lebih tinggi* → *masalahnya batu mudah lepas, debu kemana-mana* → *akhirnya batunya kamu campur aspal panas.*
>
> Fridge: *jadi dindingnya kamu isi busa tebal* → *tapi tetep aja esnya habis juga* → *jadi kamu pasang pendingin* → *eh pompanya nyala terus, sayur jadi batu* → *jadi kamu pasang termostat* → *tapi dinginnya bocor lewat celah pintu* → *jadi kamu pasang karet bermagnet.*

### Beat 3. Anatomy dissection (gradual zoom) — one layer per explanation

Camera (and narration) move from outside in, or from past to present. One sentence = one layer visible on screen.

> Road: layer after layer of asphalt, with year boards *"awal 1800-an"* (early 1800s) → *"awal 1900-an"* (early 1900s).
>
> Fridge: *di dalam kompresor ada besi yang gerak terus* (inside the compressor there's iron that keeps moving, cutaway) → *olinya ngumpul di bawah* (its oil pools at the bottom).

### Beat 4. Root-cause reveal — "Pantes..." (No wonder...)

Plain language FIRST, terms AFTER. Max 1 term per beat. The keyword is **"pantes"** (no wonder) — the moment the answer clicks. A term with no supporting visual = forbidden.

> Road: *makanya, kalo air nyusup ke bawah, batunya jadi lembek. Kulit aspalnya gak ada yang nopang lagi. Dilewatin motor terus, jalan depan rumahmu bolong tiap musim hujan.*
>
> Fridge: *pantes tukang selalu bilang diemin dulu, jangan langsung dicolok — olinya ikut ngalir ke pipa, pompanya kurang oli, pipanya bisa kesumbat.*

### Beat 5. Practical rule / payoff — 1 action sentence or twist

Viewers go home with 1 thing they can do, or 1 ironic fact stuck in their head.

> Fridge: *jadi kamu diemin kulkasnya berdiri tegak, minimal 2–4 jam. Terakhir, pas pindahan angkut berdirinya, nyalainnya kosong dulu. Pantes!*
>
> Road: *jadi lapisan hitam yang kamu lewatin itu cuma kulitnya. Kekuatannya ada di lapisan batu di bawahnya. Selamat, kamu udah bikin jalan aspal!*

### Beat 6. Cliffhanger — "Mau kita bongkar apa?"

Close with ONE hook question into the next episode, not a dry conclusion. (Exception: series finale.)

> Example: *mau kita bongkar apa?* (road ep) — *What should we tear apart next?*

## 3. Storytelling Characteristics

- **"kamu"** — the entire narration addresses viewers as the actor (*"kamu padatin dulu" / you compact it first*, *"jadi kamu diemin" / so you let it sit*).
- **No greeting, no definitions in the opening** — second 0 is scene.
- **Plain language first, terms later** — *"pompa yang nyala"* (the pump that runs) before *"kompresor"*; *"besi yang gerak"* before naming the component. Max 1 term per beat.
- **Every sentence = 1 visual action** — rain test, cross-section cut, calendar 25 → 26, year boards. If it can't be visualized, cut the sentence.
- **Supporting characters may tell story without dialogue** — the mother waiting all day, the movers who tilt the fridge.
- **Escalation = zoom (outside → in) or timeline (past → present)** — not a pile of definitions.
- **Cross-episode bridge (optional)** — 1 sentence at second 0 if the topic descends from the last episode.
- **Cliffhanger every episode** — arrive curious, stay trying along, leave with 1 rule + 1 new question.

> Never explain the main topic directly. Let viewers fail first, then dissect it together.

## 4. Emotion Tags for Text-to-Speech

Every script must carry emotion tags so the voice sounds natural, not monotonous.

> ⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** only the tags below are recognized by the skill (see `references/emotion-spring-matrix.md` — each tag triggers different spring/motion). Tags outside the list (`explaining`, `enthusiastic`, `amazed`, `energetic`, `playful`, `whispering`, `curious`, `seriously`) are NOT recognized and fall back to generic motion.

| Emotion tag | Usage (EN) | Contoh script (ID) |
| ----------- | ---------- | ------------------ |
| `[curiously]` | Curiosity, hooks, teasers, cliffhangers | *Tapi pernah nggak sih kamu mikir, panasnya sebenarnya dibuang ke mana?* |
| `[excited]` | Enthusiasm, exciting reveals, new components appearing | *Pertama, refrigeran masuk ke bagian indoor, ke komponen yang namanya evaporator!* |
| `[shocked]` | Twists, failed experiments, surprising facts | *Yang dilakuin AC cuma satu: mindahin panas dari dalam kamar ke luar rumah!* |
| `[confused]` | Wrong guesses, failed attempts, debunked misconceptions | *Soalnya AC itu nggak bikin udara dingin. Sama sekali.* |
| `[thoughtful]` | Mechanism explanations, layer dissections | *Di dalam AC ada zat khusus yang namanya refrigeran. Kerjaannya cuma bolak-balik.* |
| `[serious]` | Practical rules, must-remember facts | *Jadi aturannya simpel: indoor nyerap panas, outdoor buang panas.* |
| `[triumphant]` | Payoffs, working solutions, secrets revealed | *Pantes AC kotor bikin dompet jebol!* |
| `[sad]` | Bad effects or losses (use sparingly) | *Kulit aspalnya gak ada yang nopang lagi.* |
| `[whisper]` | Whispers or dramatic emphasis | *Panasnya... cuma pindah tempat.* |

Note: these tags direct expression for the TTS model and trigger motion in the skill. Actual effect depends on the model used.

## 5. Topic Selection Principles

Prioritize topics that:

1. Start from something easy to picture or experience (broken roads, dead fridges).
2. Have an intuitive solution that TURNS OUT to fail (feeds Beat 2).
3. Feature an object that can be dissected layer by layer visually.
4. Have a scientific or logical explanation with verified accuracy.
5. Can be pulled into a timeline (history) or zoomed anatomically (outside → in).
6. Give 1 practical rule or 1 ironic fact as payoff.

Topic categories to develop:

- Science and physics
- Everyday technology
- The human body
- Nature and environment
- History and civilization
- Transport and vehicles
- Economy and social life
- Unique Indonesian phenomena

## 6. Example `script.txt` — AC (Production Reference)

Production-standard example following the 7 beats above with a 3-round *"tapi..."* chain. Note: *"kamu"* address, plain-language-first, 1 sentence = 1 visual, *"pantes"* as the reveal, closing cliffhanger into the fridge episode. Narration stays in Indonesian — only the labels are English.

```txt
[curiously]
Kalo kamu nyalain AC, kamar kamu jadi dingin. Tapi pernah nggak sih kamu mikir, panasnya sebenarnya dibuang ke mana?

[confused]
Soalnya AC itu nggak bikin udara dingin. Sama sekali.

[shocked]
Yang dilakuin AC cuma satu: mindahin panas dari dalam kamar ke luar rumah!

[curiously]
Terus, gimana caranya?

[thoughtful]
Di dalam AC ada zat khusus yang namanya refrigeran. Kerjaannya cuma bolak-balik: nyerap panas, buang panas.

[excited]
Pertama, refrigeran masuk ke bagian indoor, ke komponen yang namanya evaporator. Di sinilah dia nyerap panas dari udara kamar. Makanya udara yang keluar jadi dingin!

[shocked]
Tapi tunggu dulu! Panas yang diserap itu nggak hilang. Panas cuma pindah tempat!

[thoughtful]
Panas itu dibawa ke bagian outdoor. Di sana ada kompresor, yang tugasnya meres refrigeran sampai tekanannya naik.

[excited]
Terus refrigeran masuk ke kondensor. Di sinilah panasnya dibuang ke udara luar! Makanya angin di dekat AC outdoor justru terasa panas!

[curiously]
Tapi kok listriknya bisa bengkak banget? Terus refrigerannya balik lagi gimana?

[thoughtful]
Lewat komponen terakhir: katup ekspansi. Tekanannya diturunin lagi sampai suhunya ikut turun, terus balik ke evaporator. Ulang terus selama AC nyala!

[serious]
Jadi aturannya simpel: indoor nyerap panas, outdoor buang panas. Filter kotor dikit aja, kerja kompresor langsung berat. Makanya tagihan naik.

[triumphant]
Pantes AC kotor bikin dompet jebol: AC itu bukan penghilang panas, tapi tukang pindah panas!

[curiously]
Nah, ada satu alat di rumah kamu yang cara kerjanya sama persis kayak AC. Kira-kira apa? Mau kita bongkar apa?
```

## 7. Output Standard for Every New Video

- Curiosity-sparking title.
- Second 0 is scene — no greeting, no definitions, no warm-up.
- Narration addresses viewers as *"kamu"* throughout.
- Storytelling follows the 7 beats (optional bridge, cold open, failed attempts, anatomy dissection, reveal, payoff, cliffhanger).
- Every sentence = 1 visual action that can be shown. What can't be visualized is cut.
- Terms introduced naturally, plain language first, max 1 term per beat.
- Escalation via zoom (outside → in) or timeline (past → present).
- Scientific info accuracy-checked.
- Every narrative section carries emotion tags ONLY from the canonical list (Section 4).
- Ideal duration 60–90 seconds.
- Script clean and ready for TTS.

**File format:** `script.txt` — content is ONLY emotion-tagged narration (no title inside; the title lives in the invocation + folder slug only). No timestamps, visual directions, scene descriptions, or editing notes. This file is strictly for Text-to-Speech.

---

## 8. Most Important Rules

Don't make videos like a teacher lecturing in class.
Make videos like someone tinkering with a friend: try, fail, tear apart, *"pantes!"* (no wonder!).

- Never open with a greeting, definition, or any warm-up.
- Don't be too formal.
- No more than 1 technical term per beat.
- Don't explain anything the visuals don't show.
- Don't force escalation if unnatural.
- Never sacrifice accuracy for sensation.
- Don't narrate monotonously.
- Make sure viewers leave with 1 new piece of knowledge + 1 new question.

> **End goal:** viewers arrive curious, stay because they try and fail along, and leave with knowledge plus curiosity for the next episode.

### 9a. Reference 1 — Asphalt Road (66 seconds)

```txt
Kalo kamu ratain tanah buat dilewatin, kamu udah punya jalan.
Tapi pas ujan, jalannya jadi bubur.
Roda nyangkut, sepatu ganti warna.
Jadi kamu taburin batu.
Tapi batunya gede-gede. Lewat situ, badan kamu ikut loncat-loncat.
Jadi tanahnya kamu padatin dulu, terus kamu tumpuk batu pecah kecil-kecil, lapis demi lapis, dipadatin lagi.
Bebannya nyebar, gak langsung neken tanah.
Ini yang orang sebut jalan makadam.
Tapi air yang ngumpul bikin bawahnya lembek.
Jadi tengah jalannya kamu bikin lebih tinggi, biar airnya lari ke selokan di pinggir.
Masalahnya, batunya jadi mudah lepas dan gampang geser.
Debunya kemana-mana.
Akhirnya, batunya kamu campur aspal panas, digelar, terus digilas sampe rata.
Jadi lapisan hitam yang kamu lewatin itu cuma kulitnya.
Kekuatannya ada di lapisan batu di bawahnya.
Makanya, kalo air nyusup ke bawah, batunya jadi lembek.
```

### 9b. Reference 2 — Dead Fridge After Moving (88 seconds)

```txt
Kalau kamu taruh es balok di lemari, kamu udah punya kulkas.
Tapi esnya cepet cair, lemarinya becek, besoknya beli es lagi.
Jadi dindingnya kamu isi busa tebal, biar panas dari luar susah masuk.
Tapi tetep aja, esnya habis juga.
Jadi kamu pasang pendingin, caranya sama kayak AC.
Ada cairan yang gampang banget nguap, diputer lewat pipa.
Pas nguap di dalam kulkas, dia nyerap panasnya terus dibuang keluar.
Yang muterinnya itu pompa di belakang, namanya kompresor.
Eh, pompanya nyala terus, semuanya jadi beku.
Sayurnya jadi batu.
Jadi kamu pasang termostat.
Kalau udah cukup dingin, pompanya mati.
Mulai anget, nyala lagi.
Pantes kulkas kadang bunyi ngeng, terus tiba-tiba sepi.
Tapi dinginnya bocor lewat celah pintu.
Jadi kamu pasang karet bermagnet di pintunya, biar nempel rapet.
Di dalam kompresor ada besi yang gerak terus, gesek-gesekan.
Biar gak cepet aus, kamu kasih oli.
Olinya ngumpul di bawah.
Masalahnya pas pindahan, kulkasnya dimiringin dan keguncang.
Olinya ikut ngalir ke pipa.
Kalau langsung dicolok, pompanya kurang oli.
Pipanya bisa kesumbat, pompanya bisa rusak.
Pantes tukang selalu bilang, diemin dulu, jangan langsung dicolok.
Jadi kamu diemin kulkasnya berdiri tegak, biar olinya turun lagi.
Minimal 2–4 jam.
Kalau sempet dibaringin, bisa semalaman.
Terakhir, pas pindahan, kamu angkut kulkasnya berdiri.
Jangan direbahin.
Nyalainnya kosong dulu, isi makanan pas udah dingin.
Selamat, kamu udah bikin kulkas.
Habis ini mau kita bongkar apa?
```

### Patterns to copy from both transcripts

- Short sentences, 1 fact per sentence, averaging 5–10 words.
- *"Jadi kamu..."* (so you...) = new solution. *"Tapi.../masalahnya..."* (but.../the problem is...) = failed solution.
- Terms always preceded by plain language (*"pompa di belakang, namanya kompresor"* / the pump at the back, called the compressor).
- *"Pantes..."* (no wonder...) = the reveal moment, appears ONCE per video, near the end.
- *"Selamat, kamu udah bikin..."* (congratulations, you've built...) = fixed payoff before the cliffhanger.

## 10. Script-First Mode (Two-Stage Pipeline)

The skill can be asked to create a script from a TITLE alone, without `vo.mp3`:

- **Stage 1** — user gives a topic title. The skill writes `script.txt` following all standards above, then STOPS. No scenes, no rendering.
- **Stage 2** — user converts the script to `vo.mp3` (TTS) and tells it to continue. The skill runs normally per `SKILL.md` (vo.mp3 + script.txt gate, duration calc, visual dissection, scaffold, render).

Why two stages: duration, pacing, and scene cuts are all derived from `vo.mp3` as the single source of truth. A script not yet locked into audio must never be animated directly.
